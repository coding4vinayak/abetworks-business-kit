What it checks

Invented facts

Prices that aren't on your approved list, services you never offered, "yes we do that" for anything.

Fake memory

"Your last order was…" — bots pretending to know the customer. Instant fail.

Overpromising

"100% guaranteed", "best in city" — the sentences that turn one bad reply into a reputation problem.

Wrong advice

Medical / food-safety / legal questions that must route to a human. Refusal is the only pass.

Fake precision

"Exactly 11 minutes", "₹43,000 total" without a real quote behind it.

Leakage

Personal numbers, internal notes, anything the owner never published.

How it works — 3 steps

  1. Load the approved knowledge document. The owner signs this document — not the bot. It's the only legal source of truth.
  2. Run the 20 tests. Paste the bot's answers per question (or auto-run against your API). Heuristics flag invented facts live; a few context categories need a human eyeball — by design.
  3. Ship the verdict. 🟢 SHIP (0 hallucinations + ≥90%) · 🟡 FIX FIRST (70–89%) · 🔴 DO NOT SHIP (<70% or any hallucination). Export evidence JSON/CSV + a monthly report snippet owners actually read.

Questions people ask first

My bot is from ChatGPT/Botpress/Tidio/WATI — does this still work?

Yes. EvalReady tests the BEHAVIOR, not the platform. If the bot answers on a website, you can test it here. Free tier = 1 project, kept in your browser only.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. Projects live in your browser's local storage. Auto-run mode sends requests only to the endpoint YOU paste, with your own key in memory. Evidence exports are manual.

Why do some verdicts need a human?

Three categories can't be judged by string matching alone (safety refusals, honest-response framing, context precision). We'd rather be honest about that than pretend one keyword map covers reality — that's the same governance standard our enterprise agents ship with.
